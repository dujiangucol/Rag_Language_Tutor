# Mandarin Survival Guide

## Tones

Mandarin has four main tones and a neutral tone. Tone changes can change meaning, so learners should practice slowly and exaggerate pitch movement at first.

- First tone: high and level, as in mā.
- Second tone: rising, as in má.
- Third tone: low dipping, as in mǎ.
- Fourth tone: sharp falling, as in mà.
- Neutral tone: short and light, as in ma.

## Basic Sentence Order

The common Mandarin sentence order is subject + time + place + verb + object. Time words often appear near the beginning of the sentence.

Examples:

- Wǒ jīntiān xué Zhōngwén. I study Chinese today.
- Tā zài xuéxiào hē kāfēi. She drinks coffee at school.

## Asking Questions

The particle "ma" turns many statements into yes-no questions.

Examples:

- Nǐ hǎo. You are well.
- Nǐ hǎo ma? Are you well?

The A-not-A pattern is another common question structure.

Example:

- Nǐ qù bù qù? Are you going or not?

## Measure Words

Mandarin usually uses measure words between numbers and nouns. The general measure word is "ge", but many nouns use specific measure words.

Examples:

- yí ge rén: one person
- sān běn shū: three books
- liǎng bēi chá: two cups of tea

## Tutor Advice

For English-speaking learners, pronunciation and tones need daily short practice. A good tutor should give minimal pairs, ask the learner to repeat, and connect each phrase to a realistic situation.
