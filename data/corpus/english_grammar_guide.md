# English Grammar Guide

## Present Simple

Use the present simple for habits, routines, general truths, schedules, and states. It often appears with adverbs such as usually, often, always, sometimes, and never.

Examples:

- I study English every morning.
- Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius.
- The train leaves at 8:15.
- She knows the answer.

Common learner issue: learners sometimes use the present continuous for permanent states. Say "I know him", not "I am knowing him".

## Present Continuous

Use the present continuous for actions happening now, temporary situations, changing trends, and near-future arrangements.

Examples:

- I am studying English right now.
- She is living in New York this semester.
- Your pronunciation is improving.
- We are meeting our tutor tomorrow.

Contrast:

- I work at a bank. This is my regular job.
- I am working late tonight. This is temporary.

## Articles

Use "a" or "an" for one non-specific singular count noun. Use "the" when the listener knows which thing you mean, or when the noun is unique in context.

Examples:

- I bought a grammar book.
- The grammar book on my desk is useful.
- She is an engineer.

Common learner issue: do not use an article before most plural general nouns. Say "Teachers need patience", not "The teachers need patience" when speaking generally.

## Modal Verbs

Modal verbs such as can, could, should, must, might, and would are followed by the base form of the verb.

Examples:

- You should review the lesson.
- She can speak Mandarin.
- They might arrive late.

Do not add "to" after most modal verbs. Say "I can go", not "I can to go".

## Conditionals

The first conditional describes realistic future possibilities: if + present simple, will + base verb.

Example:

- If I practice daily, I will improve.

The second conditional describes unreal or unlikely situations: if + past simple, would + base verb.

Example:

- If I had more time, I would read more.

The third conditional describes unreal past situations: if + past perfect, would have + past participle.

Example:

- If I had studied earlier, I would have passed the test.
